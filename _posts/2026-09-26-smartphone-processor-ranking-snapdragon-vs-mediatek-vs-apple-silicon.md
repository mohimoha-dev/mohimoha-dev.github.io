The processor (or SoC, system-on-chip) decides how fast apps open, how smooth games run, how good night photos look, and increasingly which AI features your phone can run. But chip names are confusing: *Snapdragon 8s*, *Dimensity 7300*, *A-series Pro*, *Tensor*. This guide explains the families and ranks them by tier so you can read a spec sheet with confidence.

## What's inside a phone chip

A modern SoC bundles several processors on one piece of silicon:

- **CPU**: general app speed.
- **GPU**: games and graphics.
- **NPU / AI engine**: on-device AI, photo processing, voice features.
- **ISP**: the image signal processor that turns sensor data into photos.
- **Modem**: 4G/5G connectivity (sometimes separate).

Two phones with the same CPU speed can feel very different because of the GPU, ISP and, crucially, how well the phone manages heat.

## The families at a glance

### Apple A-series

Apple designs its chips for iPhones only. The **Pro** chip in each year's Pro models is typically the fastest single-core performer in phones and very efficient. Standard iPhones often get a slightly cut-down version. Apple chips stay fast for years, which is one reason iPhones age well.

### Qualcomm Snapdragon

Used by Samsung (in many regions), Xiaomi, OnePlus, Motorola, ASUS and many others.

- **8 series (8 Elite, 8 Gen x):** flagship tier, top-class GPU for gaming.
- **8s series:** slightly trimmed flagship chips for "flagship killer" phones.
- **7 series:** upper mid-range; good balance of speed and battery.
- **6 and 4 series:** budget and entry level.

### MediaTek Dimensity

MediaTek has caught up dramatically and now powers flagships from vivo, OPPO and Xiaomi as well as many budget phones.

- **9000 series:** flagship tier, competitive with Snapdragon 8 in many tests.
- **8000 series:** high mid-range, often excellent value.
- **7000 series:** mainstream mid-range.
- **6000 series:** budget 5G.

### Google Tensor

Google's chips prioritise AI, speech and photo processing over raw benchmark numbers. They're not the fastest for gaming, but they power many of the Pixel's standout features.

### Samsung Exynos

Samsung uses Exynos in some Galaxy models and regions. Recent Exynos flagships are much closer to Snapdragon than earlier generations, and mid-range Exynos chips are common in Galaxy A models.

## Tier ranking (by typical real-world performance)

| Tier | Examples of chip families | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **S: Top flagship** | Apple A-series Pro, Snapdragon 8 Elite class, Dimensity 9000-series latest | Heavy gaming, 8K video, on-device AI |
| **A: Flagship / near-flagship** | Snapdragon 8s, previous-gen Snapdragon 8, recent Exynos flagship, Tensor | Everything, with slightly less gaming headroom |
| **B: Upper mid-range** | Snapdragon 7+ / 7 series, Dimensity 8000-series | Smooth daily use, casual-to-moderate gaming |
| **C: Mid-range** | Dimensity 7000-series, Snapdragon 6 series, mid Exynos | Everyday apps, light gaming |
| **D: Entry** | Snapdragon 4 series, Dimensity 6000-series, Helio | Calls, messaging, social media |

Within each tier, the **newest generation** usually beats the previous one, and a previous-gen flagship chip often beats a current mid-range chip.

## How to read a chip name quickly

- **Higher first digit = higher tier** (Snapdragon 8 > 7 > 6; Dimensity 9xxx > 8xxx > 7xxx).
- **"Elite", "+", "Pro", "Ultra" or "s"** signal variants; "s" in Snapdragon naming means a slightly cheaper version of the flagship.
- **Check the year.** A two-year-old flagship chip is usually still faster than a brand-new mid-range one, but it may lose out on efficiency and the newest AI features.

## Benchmarks vs. reality

Benchmark scores measure peak performance for a few minutes. Real life involves sustained load, and phones with poor cooling throttle heavily. For gaming especially, look for reviews that test **sustained performance** and temperature, not just a single score.
