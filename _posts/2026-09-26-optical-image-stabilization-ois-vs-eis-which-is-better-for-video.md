Shaky video is the fastest way to make phone footage look amateur. Every modern phone uses some form of stabilisation, but there are two very different technologies behind it: **optical (OIS)** and **electronic (EIS)**. Understanding the difference helps you choose a phone and get better footage from the one you have.

## OIS: moving hardware

Optical image stabilisation physically moves the lens (or the sensor, in "sensor-shift" designs) to counteract your hand's movement. Tiny gyroscopes detect shakes and motors shift the optics in real time.

**Strengths**

- **Sharper low-light photos:** the shutter can stay open longer without blur, so night photos are cleaner.
- **No loss of resolution:** the full frame is used.
- **Handles small, fast tremors** from your hands very well.

**Weaknesses**

- Limited range of movement; it can't smooth big motions like walking.
- Adds cost and thickness, so it's often missing from ultra-wide or budget cameras.
- The moving parts can be sensitive to strong vibration (motorbike mounts are a known risk).

## EIS: clever software

Electronic image stabilisation records a slightly larger area than the final video, then uses gyroscope data and software to shift and crop each frame so the result looks steady.

**Strengths**

- **Smooths large movements** like walking, running or filming from a car.
- Works on any camera, including ultra-wide and front cameras.
- Cheap to implement, and improves with software updates.

**Weaknesses**

- **Crops the image**, slightly narrowing the field of view and reducing detail.
- In low light, it can cause a "jelly" or smeared look because each frame has more motion blur that software can't remove.
- Can struggle with very fast movement.

## Hybrid: the best of both

Most good phones now use **OIS + EIS together**. OIS removes the fine hand jitter and keeps exposures sharp; EIS then smooths out larger movements. Many phones also offer an extra "action" or "super steady" mode that uses heavy EIS with a bigger crop, often on the ultra-wide camera.

## Quick comparison

| | OIS | EIS | OIS + EIS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low-light photos | Excellent | Weak | Excellent |
| Walking video | Fair | Good | Excellent |
| Image crop | None | Yes | Small |
| Cost | Higher | Low | Higher |
| Found on | Main cameras of mid-range and up | Almost all phones | Most flagships |

## Tips for steadier phone video

- Use the **main camera**; it usually has OIS.
- Film at **1080p or 4K at 30fps** in low light; higher frame rates often disable some stabilisation.
- Try the phone's **action/super steady mode** for running or cycling.
- Hold the phone with **both hands, elbows tucked in**, and walk heel-to-toe.
- For professional-looking movement, a **gimbal** still beats any built-in stabilisation.
