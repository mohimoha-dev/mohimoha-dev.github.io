Spyware and "stalkerware" can secretly read messages, track location, record calls or capture your screen. It's often installed by someone with physical access to your phone: a partner, ex, family member or colleague. Here's how to check for it and what to do if you find something.

## Important safety note

If you suspect someone close to you is monitoring you, **removing spyware may alert them**. If you're in an abusive situation, consider contacting a local support organisation first and use a **safe device** to plan. Your safety comes before the technical fix.

## Warning signs

- **Battery drains quickly** and the phone runs **warm** when idle
- **Data usage spikes** from unknown apps
- Unknown apps, or apps with generic names like "System Service", "Sync" or "Update"
- **Settings changed** without you: security disabled, unknown device admin
- **Google Play Protect** or security software turned off
- Someone knows things they shouldn't (messages, locations)
- On iPhone: an unknown **configuration profile** or **MDM** management

## Check on Android

### 1. Look through all installed apps

**Settings → Apps → See all apps**. Look for unfamiliar names. Some spyware hides its icon from the launcher but still appears here.

### 2. Check Device admin apps

**Settings → Security & privacy → More security settings → Device admin apps**. Only trusted apps (Find My Device, work profiles) should be listed.

### 3. Check Accessibility services

**Settings → Accessibility → Installed apps / Downloaded apps**. Spyware often abuses accessibility to read the screen.

### 4. Check notification access and special permissions

**Settings → Apps → Special app access**: review **Notification access**, **Device admin**, **Usage access** and **Install unknown apps**.

### 5. Run Google Play Protect

Play Store → profile → **Play Protect → Scan**. Make sure it's turned on.

## Check on iPhone

- **Settings → General → VPN & Device Management:** remove unknown profiles or MDM you don't recognise.
- **Settings → Privacy & Security → Safety Check:** review who has access to your location and data, and **reset access** quickly.
- Check **Apple ID devices** (Settings → [your name]) for unknown devices; sign them out.
- Update iOS. Spyware relying on jailbreaks usually breaks with updates.
- **Lockdown Mode** offers extreme protection if you're a high-risk target.

## Check your accounts too

Much "spying" happens through **accounts**, not apps:

- Google/iCloud **location sharing** (Google Maps location sharing, Find My)
- **Logged-in sessions** in Google, Apple, WhatsApp Linked Devices, Facebook, Instagram
- **Email forwarding rules**
- Change passwords and enable **2FA**

## How to remove spyware

1. Remove unknown **device admin** rights, then **uninstall** the app.
2. Remove suspicious **profiles** (iPhone).
3. Change passwords from a **different, safe device**.
4. If unsure, **back up essential data** (photos, contacts) and **factory reset**, then set up as new (don't restore full app backups).
