Face unlock works instantly on some phones in a pitch-black bedroom, while on others it gives up the moment the lights dim. The difference comes down to **how the phone sees your face**: with ordinary visible-light cameras, or with **infrared (IR)** hardware.

## Two types of face unlock

### 1. Optical (2D camera-based) face unlock

Used on many Android phones, especially mid-range and budget models.

- Uses the **front selfie camera** to capture a normal 2D image
- Compares it with your saved face using software
- Needs **visible light** to see you clearly

**In the dark:** the camera can't see enough detail. Some phones brighten the screen to light your face, but it's slower and less reliable.

**Security:** generally **less secure**. It can sometimes be fooled by photos or similar-looking people, which is why many banking apps won't accept it.

### 2. Infrared (3D structured light or IR) face unlock

Used by **iPhone Face ID** and some Android phones (e.g., certain Pixels in the past, some Huawei/Honor models).

- An **IR flood illuminator** lights your face with invisible infrared light
- A **dot projector** (on structured-light systems) projects thousands of IR dots to map depth
- An **IR camera** reads the pattern to create a **3D face map**

**In the dark:** works perfectly, because it brings its own invisible light.

**Security:** much stronger, since it measures depth and is hard to fool with photos or masks. Approved for payments and banking.

## Comparison

| | 2D optical | 3D infrared |
|---|---|---|
| Works in dark | Poor | Excellent |
| Security | Lower | High |
| Banking/payments | Usually not | Yes |
| Hardware cost | None extra | Extra sensors |
| Common on | Budget/mid Android | iPhone, some flagships |

## Tips for better face unlock on camera-based phones

- Enable **screen brightening for face unlock** in settings, if available.
- **Re-register your face** in good, even lighting.
- Add an **alternate appearance** (glasses, beard changes).
- Keep the **front camera clean**.
- Use the **fingerprint sensor** as your main method in low light or for secure apps.

## Why not all phones use infrared?

- Extra **cost and space** (notch or larger cutout)
- In-display **fingerprint sensors** are cheaper and secure, so many Android makers prioritise them instead.

## Summary

2D optical face unlock depends on visible light, so it struggles in the dark and is less secure. Infrared 3D systems like Face ID light your face invisibly and map it in depth, working in total darkness with far better security.
