Automatic mode on a modern phone is excellent. But it guesses, and sometimes it guesses wrong: blurry night shots, blown-out skies, orange indoor photos, or moving water frozen when you wanted it silky. Pro (Manual) mode lets you take control. It looks intimidating, but there are only five settings that really matter.

## Where to find Pro mode

- **Samsung:** Camera → More → Pro.
- **Xiaomi, OnePlus, OPPO, vivo:** Camera → More → Pro (or Manual).
- **Pixel:** no full Pro mode, but you can adjust exposure, white balance and shadows with sliders, and shoot RAW.
- **iPhone:** no built-in Pro mode; use apps like Halide or the Camera app's exposure and RAW (ProRAW) controls.

## The five settings that matter

### 1. ISO: sensitivity to light

Lower ISO (50–200) gives the cleanest image. Higher ISO (800+) brightens dark scenes but adds grain.
**Rule:** keep ISO as low as the light allows.

### 2. Shutter speed: how long the sensor collects light

- **Fast (1/500s and faster):** freezes action, sports, pets.
- **Normal (1/60–1/250s):** everyday handheld shots.
- **Slow (1/15s and slower):** light trails, silky water, night scenes, but you need a tripod or something steady.

### 3. White balance (WB): colour temperature

Measured in Kelvin. Lower values make the image cooler (bluer); higher values warmer.
- Around **3200K** under warm indoor bulbs.
- Around **5500K** in daylight.
- Around **6500–7000K** in shade or on cloudy days.

### 4. Focus (MF)

Manual focus is great for close-ups (macro), shooting through glass, or low light where autofocus hunts. Turn on **focus peaking** if your phone offers it; in-focus edges light up in colour.

### 5. Exposure compensation (EV)

A quick way to brighten or darken without touching ISO and shutter. Pull it down (-0.3 to -1.0) to protect bright skies.

## Starting settings for common scenes

| Scene | ISO | Shutter | Tips |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bright daylight landscape | 50–100 | 1/250s+ | EV -0.3 to keep the sky |
| Indoor portrait | 200–400 | 1/60–1/125s | WB ~3500K for warm bulbs |
| Fast action / pets | Auto or 400–800 | 1/500s+ | Burst mode |
| Night city (tripod) | 50–100 | 1–4s | Use a 2s timer |
| Light trails (tripod) | 50 | 5–15s | Low ISO, long shutter |
| Waterfall (tripod) | 50 | 1/4–1s | Silky water effect |

## Shoot RAW when it matters

RAW (DNG) files store far more information than JPEG, so you can recover shadows and highlights and fix white balance later in Lightroom or Snapseed. The trade-off is file size and editing time. Use RAW for important shots, not everyday snaps.

## Composition tips that beat any setting

- Turn on the **grid** and use the rule of thirds.
- **Clean the lens.** A fingerprint smudge causes more bad photos than any setting.
- Tap to focus on the subject, not the background.
- Use the main camera instead of digital zoom whenever you can.
