Foldable phones have moved from fragile experiments to genuine daily drivers. But they're still expensive, and the differences between models only show up after months of use: how the hinge holds up, whether the crease bothers you, and whether the big screen actually makes you more productive.

Instead of a list that goes out of date with every launch, here's how to rank any foldable on the criteria that matter, and which types of buyers each style suits.

## Two kinds of foldables

**Book-style (fold horizontally, like a book):** a normal-ish phone outside that opens into a small tablet. Examples include the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold series, Google Pixel Fold/Pro Fold, OnePlus Open, Honor Magic V and vivo X Fold lines.

**Flip-style (fold vertically, like an old flip phone):** a full-size phone that folds in half to fit small pockets. Examples include the Galaxy Z Flip, Motorola Razr and similar models.

## Criterion 1: Durability

Foldables have two weak points ordinary phones don't: the **hinge** and the **inner screen**, which uses a flexible plastic top layer instead of glass.

What to look for:

- **Hinge rating.** Manufacturers quote fold counts (often 200,000–500,000 folds, or years of typical use). More is better, but independent teardown and bend tests are more telling.
- **Water and dust resistance.** Many newer foldables have **IPX8** water resistance; some now add dust resistance (IP48 or better). Dust is the classic hinge killer, so any dust rating is a plus.
- **Factory screen protector.** Inner screens come with a protector that should **never be peeled off**. Check whether the brand offers a free replacement.
- **Repair cost and warranty.** Inner screen replacements are expensive. Look for brands offering extended care or discounted first repairs.

## Criterion 2: Crease visibility

Every foldable has a crease where the inner screen bends. How noticeable it is depends on hinge design:

- **Waterdrop hinges** let the screen curve into a teardrop shape when closed, producing a **shallower, less visible crease** and often a gap-free fold.
- Older **U-shaped hinges** create a deeper crease and leave a gap when closed.

In practice, you'll see the crease at angles and feel it when swiping across the middle. Many owners stop noticing within a week, but if it bothers you, try one in a shop under bright light first.

## Criterion 3: Multitasking

The point of a big folding screen is doing more at once. Compare:

- **Split screen and three-app layouts** (Samsung and several Chinese brands excel here).
- **Taskbar and app pairs** that relaunch your favourite combinations.
- **App continuity**, where an app moves smoothly from the cover screen to the inner screen.
- **Stylus support** on some book-style models.
- **Cover screen usefulness** on flips: can you reply to messages and run full apps without opening the phone?

Software support length matters too, because multitasking features improve with updates.

| Battery | Usually larger | Often smaller |
| Cameras | Near-flagship | Usually mid-tier |
| Price | Highest | Lower |

## Buying tips

- **Buy with a case** designed for your model's hinge.
- **Check software update promises.** Foldables are long-term investments.
- **Avoid sand and pockets full of keys.** Grit is the enemy of flexible screens.
- **Last year's model** often drops sharply in price and remains excellent.
