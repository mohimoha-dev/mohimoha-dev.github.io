Your phone shows the logo, restarts, shows the logo again, and never reaches the home screen. Or it drops you into a scary-looking recovery menu. The first instinct is to hit "factory reset", but that erases everything that isn't backed up. Try the steps below first, in order; the early ones don't touch your data.

## What causes a bootloop

- A **failed or interrupted system update**.
- A **buggy app** or launcher that crashes the system on start.
- **Full storage**, which can stop the system from booting properly.
- **A dying battery** that can't supply enough power during boot.
- **Hardware damage** after a drop or water exposure.
- On Android, **modifications** like custom ROMs, root or disabled system apps.

## Step 1: Charge it properly

Plug it into a wall charger (not a laptop) for at least 30 minutes without touching it. A deeply drained or weak battery often causes restart loops. Then try powering on.

## Step 2: Force restart

- **iPhone:** press and release Volume Up, press and release Volume Down, then hold the Side button until the Apple logo appears.
- **Most Android phones:** hold **Power + Volume Down** for 10–20 seconds.

## Step 3: Android Safe Mode

Safe Mode starts the phone with only built-in apps, which tells you whether a downloaded app is the culprit. On most phones, when the logo appears, press and hold **Volume Down** until the phone finishes booting. You'll see "Safe mode" in a corner. If it boots, uninstall recently installed apps, then restart normally.

## Step 4: Wipe cache (Android, data stays safe)

Some phones still offer **Wipe cache partition** in recovery mode. It clears temporary system files, not personal data. (Pixels and many newer phones no longer have this option.) Enter recovery with **Power + Volume Up** (varies by brand), use the volume keys to navigate and power to select. **Do not choose "Wipe data/factory reset" yet.**

## Step 5: iPhone Update mode (keeps data)

1. Connect the iPhone to a Mac (Finder) or a Windows PC (Apple Devices app or iTunes).
2. Put it into recovery mode (the force-restart sequence, but keep holding the Side button until the "computer" screen appears).
3. When the computer prompts you, choose **Update**, not Restore. It reinstalls iOS while keeping your data.

## Step 6: Android system repair tools

- **Samsung:** Smart Switch for PC offers **Emergency software recovery** and can reinstall firmware.
- **Pixel:** Google's **Android Flash Tool** or Pixel Repair Tool can reflash the system. Only the "wipe" options erase data.
- **Xiaomi, OnePlus, others:** manufacturer PC tools or an authorised service centre can reinstall firmware.

## Getting your data back before a reset

If nothing works and a reset is the only option, first check what's already safe:

- **Google Photos / iCloud Photos:** open photos.google.com or icloud.com on a computer to see what's already backed up.
- **Google account backup:** contacts, calendars and app lists sync automatically.
- **WhatsApp:** check the last Google Drive or iCloud backup date in WhatsApp on another device, if you use linked devices.

If important data isn't backed up, stop and take the phone to a professional data recovery service **before** resetting. Modern phones encrypt storage, so once the phone is reset, recovery is essentially impossible.

## Step 7: Factory reset (last resort)

Once you've accepted what might be lost, a factory reset from recovery mode (Android) or Restore (iPhone) usually fixes software bootloops. If the phone loops even after a reset, it's likely hardware, commonly the battery or motherboard.

## Prevent the next one

- Keep at least 10–15% storage free.
- Let updates finish with the phone plugged in.
- Turn on automatic backups for photos and your Google or iCloud account.
