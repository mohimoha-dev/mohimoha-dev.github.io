Your phone's battery is the one part that wears out on a schedule. Every full charge cycle takes a tiny bite out of its capacity, and after a couple of years the difference shows: the phone dies earlier, shuts down in the cold, or slows itself down to avoid sudden power drops.

Checking battery health tells you whether that's what's happening, or whether something else (an app, a setting, weak signal) is the real culprit.

## What "battery health" actually means

Battery health is usually expressed as **maximum capacity**: how much charge the battery can hold today compared with when it was new. A reading of 85% means a full charge now holds roughly 85% of the original energy.

Two other numbers are useful:

- **Cycle count**: how many full 0–100% equivalents the battery has been through. Most phone batteries are designed to keep around 80% capacity after 500–1,000 cycles, depending on the model.
- **Peak performance capability**: whether the phone is throttling performance to protect an aging battery.

## iPhone

1. Open **Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging**.
2. Check **Maximum Capacity**. Newer iPhones also show the **cycle count** (on some models under Settings → General → About).
3. Look at **Peak Performance Capability**. If it says the phone has experienced an unexpected shutdown and performance management is applied, the battery is struggling.

Apple considers a battery "consumed" around 80% maximum capacity for most models, and at that point a service message appears.

## Samsung Galaxy

1. Open the **Samsung Members** app.
2. Go to **Support → Phone diagnostics → Battery status**.
3. You'll see a rating (Good / Normal / Weak) and, on newer One UI versions, the life percentage.

Recent One UI versions also show battery information under **Settings → Battery → Battery information**, including cycle count on supported models.

## Google Pixel

On recent Pixels, go to **Settings → Battery → Battery health**. You'll see an estimate of current capacity compared with new, plus tips if it has degraded. On some models the cycle count is under **Settings → About phone → Battery information**.

## Other Android phones

- **Xiaomi / Redmi / POCO:** Settings → Battery → Battery health (on newer HyperOS builds), or the Services & feedback diagnostics.
- **OnePlus / OPPO / Realme:** Settings → Battery → Battery health on recent versions.
- **Any Android:** dial `*#*#4636#*#*` on some phones to open a testing menu with battery status (it doesn't work on every brand).

If your phone has no built-in reading, an app like AccuBattery can estimate capacity by measuring how much charge goes in over several charging sessions. It needs a few days of normal charging to become accurate.

## How to read the results

| Maximum capacity | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100% | Healthy | Nothing; focus on settings and apps if battery life is poor |
| 80–89% | Normal wear | Expect noticeably shorter days on heavy use |
| Below 80% | Worn | Replacement is usually worth it |
| Sudden shutdowns at 20–30% | Failing battery | Replace soon |

## When the battery is fine but life is still bad

If health reads above 85% and you still struggle to get through a day, the battery isn't the problem. Check **Settings → Battery** for an app using an unusual amount of power, lower the screen brightness, and look at your signal strength: a weak mobile signal is one of the biggest hidden drains.

## Replace or upgrade?

A battery replacement at an official service centre typically costs a fraction of a new phone, and it can make a two- or three-year-old phone feel new again. It's usually the smart move if the phone still gets software updates and you're happy with it otherwise.

If the phone is also out of updates or too slow, a new phone with a bigger, healthier battery makes more sense.
