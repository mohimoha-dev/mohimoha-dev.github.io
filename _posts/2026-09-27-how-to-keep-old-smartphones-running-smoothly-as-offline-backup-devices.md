An old phone in a drawer can be more than e-waste. Kept charged and set up properly, it becomes a **backup device** for emergencies, travel and everyday tasks, ready when your main phone is lost, broken or out of battery.

## Useful roles for an old phone

- **Emergency backup phone:** insert a spare SIM or eSIM in an emergency
- **Offline map and GPS device** for hiking and travel
- **Dedicated music/podcast player** for gym or car
- **2FA backup device** (authenticator codes, backup codes)
- **Home security camera** or baby monitor
- **Smart home remote** or kitchen recipe screen
- **Kids' learning or media device** (with parental controls)

## Step 1: Reset and set up clean

1. **Back up** anything on it, then **factory reset**.
2. Sign in with a **Google/Apple account** (or a dedicated secondary account).
3. Install **only the apps** you need for its role.
4. Disable or remove unnecessary pre-installed apps.

## Step 2: Offline preparation

- Download **offline maps** (Google Maps, Organic Maps).
- Save **music, podcasts and ebooks** locally.
- Store **emergency contacts**, medical info and important documents (encrypted).
- Keep a copy of **2FA backup codes**.

## Step 3: Battery care for long storage

- Store at **around 50% charge**.
- **Top up every 2–3 months** so the battery doesn't deeply discharge.
- Keep it in a **cool, dry place**.
- Check for **battery swelling** periodically. If swollen, stop using and recycle it safely.

## Step 4: Keep it secure

- Set a **screen lock**.
- Update to the **latest available software** and app versions.
- If it no longer receives **security updates**, avoid using it for banking or sensitive accounts; use it offline or for low-risk tasks.
- Enable **Find My Device**.

## Step 5: Keep it running smoothly

- Turn on **battery saver** and **dark mode**.
- Reduce **animations** (Developer options at 0.5x on Android).
- Avoid heavy apps; use **lite versions**.
- **Restart** after long storage.

## Emergency kit tip

Keep the old phone with a **charger, cable and small power bank** in your emergency or travel kit.
